# [[Definition]]

**[[Basic Electronics Engineering]]** is the branch of engineering that deals with the study, design, and application of electronic devices and circuits. It involves controlling the flow of electrons using semiconductor devices such as [[Diodes]], [[Transistors]], and [[Integrated Circuits]].

---

# [[Semiconductors]]

A **[[Semiconductor]]** is a material whose electrical conductivity lies between that of a conductor and an insulator.

Examples

- [[Silicon]]
- [[Germanium]]

Applications

- Diodes
- Transistors
- Integrated Circuits
- Solar Cells

---

## [[Intrinsic Semiconductor]]

A pure semiconductor without impurities.

Characteristics

- Equal number of electrons and holes
- Low conductivity

---

## [[Extrinsic Semiconductor]]

A semiconductor whose conductivity is increased by adding impurities.

Types

- [[P-Type Semiconductor]]
- [[N-Type Semiconductor]]

---

## [[P-Type Semiconductor]]

Formed by adding **trivalent impurities**.

Majority Charge Carriers

- Holes

Minority Charge Carriers

- Electrons

---

## [[N-Type Semiconductor]]

Formed by adding **pentavalent impurities**.

Majority Charge Carriers

- Electrons

Minority Charge Carriers

- Holes

---

# [[PN Junction Diode]]

A **[[PN Junction Diode]]** is formed by joining P-type and N-type semiconductors.

Functions

- Allows current in one direction.
- Blocks current in the opposite direction.

Applications

- Rectifiers
- Clippers
- Clampers
- Switching Circuits

---

## [[Forward Bias]]

- Positive terminal connected to P-side.
- Negative terminal connected to N-side.
- Diode conducts current.

---

## [[Reverse Bias]]

- Positive terminal connected to N-side.
- Negative terminal connected to P-side.
- Diode blocks current.

---

# [[Rectifiers]]

A **[[Rectifier]]** converts [[Alternating Current]] (AC) into [[Direct Current]] (DC).

Types

- [[Half Wave Rectifier]]
- [[Full Wave Rectifier]]
- [[Bridge Rectifier]]

---

## [[Half Wave Rectifier]]

Uses one diode.

Advantages

- Simple
- Low cost

Disadvantages

- Low efficiency
- High ripple

---

## [[Full Wave Rectifier]]

Uses two diodes with a center-tapped transformer.

Advantages

- Higher efficiency
- Lower ripple

---

## [[Bridge Rectifier]]

Uses four diodes.

Advantages

- No center-tapped transformer required
- High efficiency

---

# [[Problem Solving Strategy]]

1. Identify the electronic device.
2. Determine its operating region.
3. Apply the appropriate circuit laws.
4. Calculate voltages and currents.
5. Verify the output.
6. Check practical limitations.

---

# [[Common Mistakes]]

- Confusing P-type and N-type semiconductors.
- Reversing diode bias conditions.
- Mixing AC and DC analysis.
- Confusing BJT and FET operation.
- Forgetting logic gate truth tables.
- Using incorrect transistor terminal connections.

**[[Basic Electronics Engineering]]** provides the foundation for modern electronic systems. It covers the study of [[Semiconductors]], [[Diodes]], [[Rectifiers]], [[Transistors]], [[Amplifiers]], [[Operational Amplifiers]], [[Logic Gates]], [[Digital Electronics]], and [[Integrated Circuits]]. These concepts form the basis of computers, communication systems, embedded devices, automation, and modern consumer electronics.
