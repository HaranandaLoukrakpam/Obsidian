# [[Definition]]

**[[Applied Statistics]]** is the branch of [[Statistics]] that focuses on using statistical methods to solve **real-world problems**. It involves collecting, organizing, analyzing, interpreting, and presenting data to support decision-making in various fields such as [[Engineering]], [[Business]], [[Medicine]], [[Economics]], [[Computer Science]], and [[Artificial Intelligence]].

Unlike [[Theoretical Statistics]], which develops statistical methods, [[Applied Statistics]] emphasizes their practical implementation.

---

# [[Objectives of Applied Statistics]]

The main objectives of [[Applied Statistics]] are to:

- Collect reliable data.
- Organize and summarize information.
- Analyze data to identify patterns.
- Draw meaningful conclusions.
- Make predictions based on data.
- Support decision-making under uncertainty.

---

# [[Importance of Applied Statistics]]

[[Applied Statistics]] is important because it helps:

- Make evidence-based decisions.
- Improve product quality.
- Forecast future trends.
- Optimize business operations.
- Analyze scientific experiments.
- Build predictive models.
- Reduce uncertainty and risk.

# [[Basic Terminology]]

## [[Population]]

The **[[Population]]** is the complete collection of individuals, objects, or observations being studied.

Example

- All students in a university.
- Every manufactured bulb in a factory.

---

## [[Sample]]

A **[[Sample]]** is a subset of the [[Population]] selected for analysis.

Example

- 100 students selected from a university.

---

## [[Parameter]]

A **[[Parameter]]** is a numerical characteristic of a [[Population]].

Examples

- Population Mean ($\mu$)
- Population Variance ($\sigma^2$)
- Population Standard Deviation ($\sigma$)

---

## [[Statistic]]

A **[[Statistic]]** is a numerical characteristic calculated from a [[Sample]].

Examples

- Sample Mean ($\bar{x}$)
- Sample Variance ($s^2$)
- Sample Standard Deviation ($s$)

---

## [[Variable]]

A [[Variable]] is any measurable characteristic that can take different values.

Examples

- Age
- Height
- Income
- Temperature

# [[Measures of Central Tendency]]

These measures describe the center of a dataset.

## [[Arithmetic Mean]]

$$
\bar{x}
=
\frac{\sum x}{n}
$$

---

## [[Median]]

The middle value of ordered observations.

---

## [[Mode]]

The observation occurring most frequently.

---

# [[Measures of Dispersion]]

These describe the spread of data.

## [[Range]]

$$
Range
=
Maximum-Minimum
$$

---

## [[Variance]]

Population

$$
\sigma^2
=
\frac{\sum(x-\mu)^2}{N}
$$

Sample

$$
s^2
=
\frac{\sum(x-\bar{x})^2}{n-1}
$$

---

## [[Standard Deviation]]

Population

$$
\sigma
=
\sqrt{\sigma^2}
$$

Sample

$$
s
=
\sqrt{s^2}
$$

---

## [[Coefficient of Variation]]

Measures relative variation.

$$
CV
=
\frac{\sigma}{\mu}
\times100\%
$$

# [[Probability Distributions]]

## [[Normal Distribution]]

Properties

- Bell-shaped curve
- Symmetric
- Mean = Median = Mode

Applications

- Measurement errors
- Heights
- Test scores

---

## [[Binomial Distribution]]

Used when

- Fixed number of trials
- Two possible outcomes
- Independent trials

Applications

- Quality inspection
- Pass/Fail experiments

---

## [[Poisson Distribution]]

Used to model rare events occurring within a fixed interval.

Applications

- Machine failures
- Traffic accidents
- Phone calls

---

## [[Uniform Distribution]]

Every outcome has equal probability.

Applications

- Random number generation
- Computer simulations

---

# [[Sampling Techniques]]

## [[Simple Random Sampling]]

Each member has an equal probability of selection.

---

## [[Systematic Sampling]]

Every $k^{th}$ observation is selected.

---

## [[Stratified Sampling]]

Population divided into homogeneous groups before sampling.

---

## [[Cluster Sampling]]

Population divided into clusters, and selected clusters are studied.

# [[Estimation]]

## [[Point Estimation]]

Uses a single value to estimate a population parameter.

Example

Using $\bar{x}$ to estimate $\mu$.

---

## [[Interval Estimation]]

Provides a range within which the population parameter is expected to lie.

Example

[[Confidence Interval]]

# [[Hypothesis Testing]]

[[Hypothesis Testing]] is used to determine whether evidence supports a claim about a population.

Steps

1. State the [[Null Hypothesis]] ($H_0$).
2. State the [[Alternative Hypothesis]] ($H_1$).
3. Choose the significance level.
4. Compute the test statistic.
5. Make a decision.

# [[Correlation]]

Measures the strength and direction of the relationship between two variables.

$$
-1
\le
r
\le
1
$$

Interpretation

- $r=1$ → Perfect Positive Correlation
- $r=-1$ → Perfect Negative Correlation
- $r=0$ → No Linear Relationship

# [[Problem Solving Strategy]]

1. Define the problem.
2. Collect appropriate data.
3. Organize the data.
4. Choose suitable statistical methods.
5. Analyze the results.
6. Interpret the findings.
7. Draw conclusions and make recommendations.

---

# [[Common Mistakes]]

- Using biased samples.
- Confusing [[Population]] with [[Sample]].
- Assuming [[Correlation]] implies causation.
- Choosing an inappropriate statistical test.
- Ignoring assumptions of statistical models.
- Misinterpreting probability values.
- Drawing conclusions from insufficient data.

# [[Summary]]

**[[Applied Statistics]]** applies statistical principles to real-world problems across engineering, science, business, healthcare, economics, and computer science. It combines techniques from [[Probability]], [[Descriptive Statistics]], [[Inferential Statistics]], [[Sampling]], [[Regression Analysis]], and [[Hypothesis Testing]] to analyze data, make predictions, improve decision-making, and solve practical problems. It forms the foundation of modern fields such as [[Data Science]], [[Machine Learning]], [[Artificial Intelligence]], and [[Business Analytics]].
